# Financial Markets (Global)

Financial markets are the vital systems that allow society to manage risk and foster enterprise. Rather than being purely about numbers, this field is about the institutions, technology, and human behaviors that enable us to trade value across time and space.

## Core Concepts

### 1. Risk Management and Insurance
At the heart of finance is the ability to handle uncertainty. Through insurance and diversification, individuals and organizations can protect themselves against catastrophic losses. By pooling risks together, the financial system provides a safety net that encourages innovation and long-term planning.

### 2. Market Efficiency and Behavioral Finance
This course examines the tension between the "Efficient Market Hypothesis"—the idea that prices always reflect all available information—and "Behavioral Finance," which studies how human psychology and biases lead to market bubbles and crashes. Understanding the balance between logic and emotion is key to navigating global markets.

### 3. Debt, Equity, and Real Estate
We explore the fundamental building blocks of investment. This includes how stocks represent ownership in a company's future, how bonds allow for the borrowing of capital, and how real estate markets function as a primary source of wealth and stability for individuals and the broader economy.

### 4. The Role of Central Banking
Central banks like the Federal Reserve act as the "lenders of last resort." They manage the money supply, influence interest rates, and implement policies to control inflation. Their decisions have a profound impact on everything from the cost of a home mortgage to the global employment rate.

### 5. Innovation and Crisis
Financial history is a cycle of innovation followed by regulation. We analyze how new financial instruments are created to solve problems, but can also lead to systemic risks if not properly understood or governed. Studying past crises, such as the 2008 housing bubble, provides essential lessons for future stability.

## Purpose and Vision
The ultimate goal of studying financial markets is to understand how to build a more resilient and inclusive society. By mastering these tools, we can better allocate resources to where they are needed most, ensuring that capital serves the needs of humanity.
